CLINICAL REFERENCE

Emergency Protocol

FIRST in the event of an emergency, STOP IV INFUSION!! Assess the client's situation and call 911 if indicated. AED is available for immediate use if needed. Vitals should be continuously monitored! In the storage cabinet, set aside a labeled bin "EMERGENCY MEDICINES" and include those listed below.

Epinephrine for Anaphylaxis

  • Break the top of the ampule and using a filter needle, draw up epinephrine (1mg/ml) 0.3mL.
  • Replace the filter needle with IM injection needle and prepare for IM injection in the outer thigh. Repeat in 5 minutes if indicated.

    Continue to assess clients and document nursing interventions with a timeline.

  • May hang a new line of 250/500/1000mL plain LR or NS at bolus rate. DO NOT restart the previous line that has compounded ingredients.

Diphenhydramine for rash, swelling, or itching

  • Pop cap, draw up 25-50mg diphenhydramine according to client weight. Prepare for IM injection or give through IV site that has been flushed with 10mL of Saline.

    Continue to assess clients and document nursing interventions with a timeline.

  • May hang a new line of 250/500/1000mL plain LR or NS at bolus rate. DO NOT restart the previous line that has compounded ingredients.

Oral Benadryl for mild reactions

  • Dose per carton recommendations.
  • Provide plenty of PO fluids.

Ammonia Smelling Salts for syncope

  • Rip open the packet and place it in front of the client's nose. Check the client's blood glucose level via glucometer and administer crackers/juice if indicated.

In an emergency, the nurse must submit an incident report.
